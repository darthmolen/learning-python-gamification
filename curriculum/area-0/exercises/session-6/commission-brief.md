# The Commission

**Session 6. This is the whole session.** No teaching, no new orders, no worked
example. Somebody has asked you for a thing and you are going to build it.

Pick **one**. All three are the same difficulty. Pick the one you actually want.

---

## A — The Nameplate

Somebody wants a nameplate they can hang on a door.

It asks who it is for, asks how big, and draws a framed plate with the name in it.

*Extra credit if you feel like it:* a second line of smaller text, a chosen colour,
a border that is not a rectangle.

## B — The Banner

Somebody wants a banner in the Minecraft sense: a tall rectangle with a pattern
on it, in colours they choose.

It asks for two colours and a size, and draws the banner with at least two bands
or stripes on it.

*Extra credit if you feel like it:* a third colour, a chevron instead of a stripe,
a pole to hang it from.

## C — The Blueprint

Somebody wants the floorplan of a base drawn to scale before they build it.

It asks for a width and a depth **in blocks**, draws the outline with each block
worth 20 pixels on screen, and prints how many blocks of floor and how many
blocks of wall the build will cost.

*Extra credit if you feel like it:* a door gap in one wall, an interior wall,
the numbers written onto the drawing.

---

## What every commission must do

These are not suggestions. Check each one before you say you are finished.

- [ ] It asks the person at least **two** questions with `input`
- [ ] At least one answer gets converted with `int` or `float`, and you can say why
- [ ] It uses at least **three** variables with names that mean something
- [ ] It prints at least one number it **worked out**, not one it was told
- [ ] At least one `print` uses an **f-string**
- [ ] It draws something that is recognisably the thing that was asked for
- [ ] It does not crash on a sensible answer
- [ ] It runs from a fresh terminal with `py -3.14 <yourfile>.py`

## The last one, which is the real one

**It has to run on your dad's machine, from the file, with him typing the command.**

Not on yours, with the window already open, with you nudging it. His machine, his
terminal, your file, cold.

This is a rehearsal. In about six weeks there is a boss fight whose entire win
condition is that sentence, and it is the one that every learn-to-code website in
the world fails at. You are getting an early look at it.

## What breaks it

Nothing. There is no failing this session.

If it crashes on his machine, that is not a fail — that is the single most useful
piece of information the session can produce, and you write it down in the
Journal under *what broke* and it is worth exactly as much as a success.
