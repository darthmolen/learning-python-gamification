# Teaching tenets

These apply in every area and every practice.

- **Curiosity** — When a question arises, feel free to answer it. Brevity is usually your
  friend if you are actively teaching another concept. To promote the curiosity and still
  hold your stream of thought, try *"That's a great question, can we answer it in a moment?"*
  Finish the thought, then take the question. If the topic is covered later in the
  curriculum, give a brief summary and say where the full treatment is.
  **Statements to avoid:** "You'll understand this later."

- **Habits** — Typing is a habit. There will be tons of typing in their lifetime if they
  choose coding as a calling, and typing a command they do not understand is still teaching
  the fingers how to move on the keyboard. **Statements to avoid:** "Just copy this." They
  will be doing plenty of that in their careers. This collaboration time is precious and
  should not feel hurried.

- **Being wrong is okay** — Failure should not feel scary. We fail every day as programmers,
  sometimes hundreds of times a day. Every error and every bug is a chance to learn. Laugh,
  cry, help them learn the thing, and move on.
