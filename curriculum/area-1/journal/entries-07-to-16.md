# The Journal, entries 07–16

Area 0 established the habit. This file says what changes, what does not, and what to say
on the two nights where the Journal does something it has not done before.

**What does not change:** one entry per session, four prompts, ten XP paid for substance
rather than existence (§5.6), your reply written underneath the same evening. The
template is `../TEMPLATE.md` and the rubric is `../../area-0/dm-guide.md` §6.

**Still plain markdown, still not in git.** That arrives at Area 2a on schedule, week 6.
Area 0's six entries plus these ten become the first real commit in his repository, which
is a considerably better first commit than an empty README.

---

## The one thing that is new: the forecast gets read back

Area 0's fourth prompt — *what will break next time* — was written and then filed. From
entry 07 it is **read out loud at the start of the following session**, before anything
else happens, as its own one-minute beat.

§5.6 is blunt about why: *a forecast nobody checks is a wish.*

Two sentences is the whole beat.

> "Last time you wrote that the loop-inside-a-loop indentation would get you. Here is what
> actually happened tonight: it did, twice, in the first ten minutes."

Then move on. Do not turn it into a lesson. The value is that it happened at all.

**When he is right, say so clearly.** In this area he will be right often, because the
failures are repetitive — the hang, the off-by-one, the reset accumulator. Being able to
predict your own mistakes is a real professional skill and almost nobody ever tells a
child they have it.

**When he is wrong, that is also worth a sentence.** "You thought `while` would be the
hard part and it was fine. What was actually hard?" That question is worth more than the
forecast was.

## What to say before entry 07

Once, at the end of session 1 of this area. Thirty seconds.

> "Same Journal, same ten XP, one change. From tonight, the last question — what will
> break next time — gets read out at the start of the next session. Out loud. So write
> something you actually think, not something that sounds good. If you get it right I
> will say so."

That is all. Do not oversell it.

## Before the boss

§5.6: he rereads the Journal **from the start of the area** before every boss fight. That
is entries 07 through 15, at the top of session 10, and it takes about five minutes.

This is the moment the mechanic earns its keep, and it is worth telling him what to look
for rather than leaving it as homework:

> "Read your own *what broke* answers. Not for the code — for the pattern. The same thing
> got you three times this area. Find it before it gets you a fourth time tonight."

There is usually exactly one such thing and he will find it. Reset accumulators, or
indentation, or an off-by-one at a boundary.

**Then say the other half**, because it is the part he cannot see on his own:

> "Now read entry 07 again. Six weeks ago. Notice how easy it is."

That feeling — reading your own writing from six weeks ago and finding it obvious — is
the best evidence he will ever get that he is getting better at this, and no score,
badge, or adult telling him so can produce it.

## Scoring, unchanged, with Area 1 examples

Ten XP, paid for substance. Empty prompts pay nothing and he was told so in week one.

| Prompt | Pays nothing | Pays |
|---|---|---|
| What I built | "a spiral" | "a spiral of 20 lines, each 5 longer than the last, printed 1150 ink at the end" |
| What broke | "nothing" | "my hexagon had five sides and there was no error — I had to print the count to see it" |
| What I'd do differently | "nothing" | "work the turn out from the number of sides instead of typing 60" |
| What will break next time | "dunno" | "the loop inside a loop — I still can't tell which lines belong to which" |

**"What broke: nothing" is harder to challenge this area and more often false.** The
honest push, once:

> "The hexagon had five sides for a while. What did the computer say about that?"

Nothing. It said nothing. That is the entry.

Push once, gently, then let it go. Pushing twice turns the Journal into homework and it
will die.
