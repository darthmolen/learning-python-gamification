# How to play

This page is about the game — the part that keeps score. The curriculum is the real thing and
the game is wrapped around it. If you want the Python, read *How to learn* above.

## How the game sees the curriculum

The game does not score everything you do. It scores some of it.

An **area** is a chunk of the curriculum with a number and a subject. Inside an area are the
practices, in order, and that order is the curriculum's. Some of those practices carry a
**quest**.

A quest is an exercise the game has decided to score. It is the same exercise either way — the
same brief, the same starter, the same hidden test. What the quest adds is a difficulty number
and a payout.

**So a quest is not extra work, and a practice without one is not less work.** It is the same
curriculum, and the game is only keeping score on part of it. If you only ever did the quests
you would be skipping practices that the ones after them depend on.

## Difficulty and XP

Every quest carries a **Difficulty Class**, on the 5 to 30 scale you already know from the
dice. It is set against how hard the thing is *for you, now*, not against Python in the
abstract.

XP comes from the DC. A harder thing pays more. Anything at DC 20 or above is marked with a
warning, and that mark is information rather than a dare.

Progress is always shown as **cleared out of total** — `3 of 6` — because a score with no
denominator tells you how far you have come and nothing about how far is left. When an area is
still being written the total wears a tilde, `3 of ~5`, because it is an estimate and saying so
is more honest than rounding it into a fact.

## Medals

Clearing a quest is one thing. **Medals** are the other things you can do with it.

They are earned one at a time and they stack up over replays. Going back to a quest you have
already cleared to take a medal off it is a normal move and not a sign you did it wrong the
first time. `game/medals.md` lists what each one is worth.

## Bosses

Each area ends with a **boss**. There is no starter file and there are no hints — a blank
file, a specification, and a choice of framings that change the words on the screen and not
the program underneath.

Three cleared quests unlock the boss, and **you choose which three.** You may also just walk
up to a boss you have not unlocked and try it. Beating it early skips the rest of the area and
pays a bonus. Losing costs you nothing but the attempt, and the attempt is kept.

## Invasions and scars

**Invasions** are short drills on ideas you met a while ago, scheduled to arrive just as you
are about to forget them. Getting one wrong is not a penalty. It tells the scheduler to bring
that idea back sooner.

**Scars** are failures, kept on purpose and shown on purpose. Nothing here hides that
something went wrong, because the record of what broke is more useful than a clean sheet.

## Working on your own, and what happens at a boss

*How to work on your own* covers the curriculum half — which practices survive being done alone
and which do not. This is the part it cannot say, because that page is written to be read with
the game switched off.

**Almost everything scores itself with nobody watching.** Hidden tests run on the server. A
pushed repository gets cloned and tested. Your journal is read out of your own git log. None of
it waits for a person, and none of it happens faster because somebody is in the room. Work on a
Wednesday, and it counts on Wednesday.

**Bosses are the exception, and they do not block you.** Every boss needs a sign-off: somebody
other than you presses the button. So do it anyway — take the boss, work it, push it, and write
your teach-back. The attempt is recorded straight away and waits in the queue as *not passed
yet*. Whoever holds the DM seat grants it later, from wherever they are, and the XP lands then.

Nothing expires. Nothing is lost by finishing at ten at night.

**Say what you did while it is fresh.** The sign-off is a person reading your explanation, so the
explanation is the work. Write it when you finish rather than three days later when the button
gets pressed — you will never remember it as well again, and a good teach-back is most of what
makes the sign-off quick.

**A refusal is not a scolding.** If the explanation was not good enough, the attempt stays on the
record as one that did not pass and you go again. That is the same rule that keeps every scar:
what broke is more useful than a clean sheet.

## The practice checkbox is scaffolding

The tick beside each practice is yours to set. Nothing checks it and nothing is unlocked by
it — it is there so you can see where you are, and no quest is gated behind it.

It is also **scaffolding**, named as such. It lives in the game's own bookkeeping and it dies
with the game. The thing that outlives all of this is your journal and your repository, which
is the right way round.
